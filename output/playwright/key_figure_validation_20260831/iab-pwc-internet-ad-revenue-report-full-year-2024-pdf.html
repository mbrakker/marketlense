Executive summary

U.S. internet advertising revenue returned to robust growth in fiscal year 2024, reaching a record $258.6 billion after increasing 14.9% year over year. Growth remained positive in every quarter, from 15.7% in Q1 to 14.3% in Q4, indicating sustained momentum rather than a single-period surge. Scale still rests with search, which generated $102.9 billion, while digital video was the fastest-growing major format at 19.2% year-over-year growth. Programmatic revenue reached $134.8 billion, with open real-time bidding accounting for 61% of programmatic revenue and direct deals 39%. Social media and retail media network revenues also expanded sharply, reaching $88.8 billion and $53.7 billion, respectively. The strategic implication is a market combining broad digital demand with faster growth in video, automated buying, social, and commerce media, while the top 10 companies still accounted for 80.8% of revenue; the report cautions that its figures rely partly on company-reported and public data and were not independently audited or validated by PwC.

05. Expert view

MarketBearing expert view.

Our team's perspective translates the report into practical implications for business, brand and digital leaders.

MB

MarketBearing analysis

U.S. internet advertising reached $258.6 billion in fiscal year 2024, but the strongest growth was concentrated in newer or expanding parts of the market. Digital video grew 19.2% year over year, programmatic 18.0%, and retail media networks 23.0%, compared with 14.9% for internet advertising overall, while search remained the largest segment at $102.9 billion. The implication is a portfolio tension between preserving search scale and allocating attention to faster-growing video, programmatic, and commerce media activity. These shifts also represent different operating propositions rather than a single market movement. Open real-time bidding accounted for 61% of programmatic revenue, while retail media expansion was associated with first-party data and closed-loop reporting, and the report frames AI, privacy changes, creators, streaming, and commerce integration as simultaneous forces reshaping advertising models. Leaders should therefore evaluate formats, buying methods, and retail media capabilities separately, while keeping concentration in view: the top 10 companies still accounted for 80.8% of U.S. internet advertising revenue in fiscal year 2024.

Search still carries the scale in U.S. internet advertising. Digital video is taking more of the growth. That channel tension is one representative lens from the IAB/PwC Internet Ad Revenue Report, Full-year 2024 results, rather than a summary of the report as a whole. In fiscal year 2024, search generated $102.9 billion and represented 39.8% of internet advertising revenue. Digital video generated $62.1 billion, while recording 19.2% year-over-year growth, the fastest rate among the major formats identified in the report. For ecommerce and digital operators, the implication is a portfolio tradeoff rather than a simple channel replacement. Search remains the largest segment, preserving its role as a scaled part of the market. Video is expanding faster, creating a distinct growth profile within the same advertising environment. Treating both channels as interchangeable would obscure that difference. The broader market context reinforces why the distinction matters. Internet advertising returned to robust growth in fiscal year 2024, but the report also finds that revenue remained highly concentrated among the largest companies. Channel decisions therefore sit within a market where scale and growth are distributed differently across formats and platforms. The useful planning lens is to separate the question of where existing scale sits from where expansion is occurring. The report’s data suggests those answers are not identical.
