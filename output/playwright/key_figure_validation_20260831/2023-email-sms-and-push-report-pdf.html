01. Overview

2023 Email Sms And Push Report

Executive summary of the source report, shaped for fast review and downstream decision-making.

Executive summary

The clearest executive priority is behavior-triggered automation: automated email generated 41% of all email orders from only 2% of sends, while automated SMS generated 26% of SMS orders from 13% of sends and automated push generated 21% of push orders from 3%. Promotional email still scaled in 2023, with nearly 23 billion campaigns sent, up 33% year over year and producing 17% more purchases; open rate rose from 22.9% in 2022 to 25.1% in 2023 and click rate from 1.2% to 1.5%. Conversion remained constrained for campaign email, declining from 0.08% to 0.07% despite the higher engagement measures. SMS and push expanded the addressable channel mix, with 266 million push messages generating more than $13 million in sales, while the report estimates that $47 million could have been left unrealized without SMS and push in 2023. For 2024, the report recommends coordinated email, SMS, and push workflows, beginning with cart-abandonment, browse-abandonment, and welcome automations and supported by stronger opt-in growth. The main limitation is that conversion rates use attributed orders divided by messages sent, so the findings do not establish incremental causality.

05. Expert view

MarketBearing expert view.

Our team's perspective translates the report into practical implications for business, brand and digital leaders.

MB

MarketBearing analysis

The 2023 data show a clear allocation tradeoff: promotional email sends increased 33% year over year and purchases rose 17%, yet conversion declined from 0.08% in 2022 to 0.07% in 2023. By contrast, automated email generated 41% of email orders from 2% of sends and converted at nearly four times the rate of campaign email. Campaigns therefore remain part of the growth mix, but the evidence supports making behavior-triggered journeys the primary performance focus rather than relying on message-volume expansion. The same pattern across email, SMS, and push supports coordinated channel orchestration rather than isolated channel growth. The report identifies approximately half a million orders and $47 million as sales left on the table without SMS and push in 2023, while its guidance emphasizes cross-channel workflows, stronger opt-in growth, and targeted use cases. Leaders should evaluate these channels through both commercial contribution and the relevant engagement or conversion objective, since industry benchmarks vary materially and conversion rates are affected by send volume and the use of behavior-based automation.
in LinkedIn-ready post
Omnisend’s Email, SMS, and Push Marketing Statistics for Ecommerce in 2024 is a broad report covering channel performance and planning guidance. One representative angle stands out: behavior-triggered automation appears to deserve priority over simply increasing promotional email volume. In 2023, automated emails generated 41% of all email orders from only 2% of email sends, with a 1.9% conversion rate. Omnisend’s methodology distinguishes campaigns, which brands manually send for promotions, from automations, which are triggered by user behavior. That distinction makes the finding operationally relevant: automation connects the message to an observed customer action rather than relying only on broad distribution. Promotional campaigns still showed stronger engagement in 2023, even as conversion declined slightly amid substantial send growth. The report’s data therefore supports a sequencing decision, not an either-or conclusion: maintain campaign activity while directing greater performance attention toward journeys that respond to customer intent. Omnisend’s 2024 guidance recommends beginning with cart-abandonment, browse-abandonment, and welcome messages, then combining email, SMS, and push within unified workflows. It also points to stronger opt-in growth and targeted SMS use cases as part of channel expansion. For ecommerce teams, the practical implication is to establish priority customer triggers before depending on broader campaign growth to deliver more orders. The planning question is simple: which behavior should trigger the next message?
