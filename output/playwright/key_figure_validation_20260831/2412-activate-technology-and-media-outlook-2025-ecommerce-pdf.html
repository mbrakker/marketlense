Executive summary

Executives should treat eCommerce as an integrated system spanning physical retail, marketplaces, retail media, discovery, and operating capabilities. Global eCommerce sales are forecast to rise from $7.2 trillion in 2024E to $10.4 trillion in 2028E, while physical retail is forecast to increase from $21.8 trillion to $25.1 trillion, with combined sales projected to surpass $35 trillion in 2028E. Marketplace participation is central to the model: third-party sales accounted for 83% of the $4.0 trillion in 2023 global online GMV generated by the top 10 eCommerce companies, and nine of the top 10 operated a marketplace. U.S. retail media revenue is forecast to nearly double from $54 billion in 2024E to $101 billion in 2028E, requiring capabilities across ad serving, monetization, ad sales, media activation, and sponsored and organic product discovery. Shopping discovery is spreading across retail websites, social platforms, traditional search, brand-owned stores, marketplaces, and generative AI, with generative AI usage highest among shoppers aged 18–34 at 51%. Brands cannot rely on direct-to-consumer channels alone to scale and will need coordinated decisions across marketplaces, pricing, demand and supply planning, retail media, customer experience, and data analytics. Consumer survey findings apply to U.S. adults aged 18 or older who shopped online at least once in the preceding 12 months.

05. Expert view

MarketBearing expert view.

Our team's perspective translates the report into practical implications for business, brand and digital leaders.

MB

MarketBearing analysis

Commerce strategy is becoming platform-centered, not simply digital-first. The top 10 eCommerce companies generated 62% of global online GMV in 2023, while third-party marketplace sales accounted for 83% of the top 10’s online GMV and nine of those players operated marketplaces. Leaders should therefore treat marketplace participation, retail media, and the on-site shopping experience as connected capabilities, not separate channel decisions. Discovery is also spreading across retail, social, search, and generative AI, increasing the importance of coordinated channel management. Generative AI search usage for shopping inspiration and research reached 51% among U.S. online shoppers aged 18–34 in 2024 consumer research, while the report describes traditional search as being eclipsed among younger shoppers. The decision requirement is to align channel reach, marketing allocation, portfolio presentation, and shopper journeys across these environments rather than optimize D2C in isolation. This coordination must include physical retail because the forecast places more than $3 trillion of growth dollars in each of global eCommerce and physical retail through 2028E, with total sales reaching $35.4 trillion. Retail media adds a monetization and discovery layer, with U.S. revenue forecast to rise from $54 billion in 2024E to $101 billion in 2028E, while value, loyalty, immersive commerce, and flexible fulfillment are also identified as connected elements of the shopper proposition. Senior teams should consequently design commerce enablement around the full multi-channel system, with the framework treated as directional rather than exhaustive.
in LinkedIn-ready post
Marketplace participation is not peripheral to eCommerce strategy. In Activate Consulting’s Activate Technology & Media Outlook 2025: eCommerce, one representative lens is the concentration of online commerce among marketplace-led businesses. In 2023, third-party marketplace sales accounted for 83% of the $4.0 trillion in global online GMV generated by the top 10 eCommerce companies. Nine of the 10 operated a marketplace. That is a material distinction: leading platforms are not relying solely on owned inventory or direct retail models. They are enabling a broader seller ecosystem. The implication for brands is that direct-to-consumer cannot be treated as the only path to scale. Activate identifies marketplaces, other online retailers, and D2C platforms as part of the multi-channel sales environment, with cross-channel challenges spanning branding, demand and supply planning, pricing, retail media, customer experience, and data analytics. This creates a management tradeoff. Marketplace participation can extend reach within a concentrated ecosystem, but it also requires decisions about assortment, seller relationships, pricing, brand control, and channel economics. Those decisions cannot sit apart from the rest of the commerce operating model. The relevant question is therefore less whether a brand should participate in marketplaces, and more how marketplace requirements will be coordinated with its other channels without fragmenting the customer, commercial, and operational decisions that support growth.
