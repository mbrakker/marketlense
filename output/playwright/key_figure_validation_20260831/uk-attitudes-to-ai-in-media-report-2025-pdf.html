01. Overview

Uk Attitudes To Ai In Media Report 2025

Executive summary of the source report, shaped for fast review and downstream decision-making.

Executive summary

In Great Britain, generative AI in media faces a trust and legitimacy gap: concern about misinformation, low confidence in AI-generated news, and demand for disclosure and stronger regulation outweigh broad public comfort, with attitudes differing by generation. During YouGov fieldwork from December 16, 2024 to January 2, 2025, 70% said they trusted AI-generated news less than human-created news; only 19% trusted it equally or more, versus 37% across all surveyed markets. Disclosure and regulatory assurance are prominent expectations: 86% considered disclosure of generative AI use important, while 70% said there were not enough regulations, compared with 8% who said there were enough. Misinformation and deepfakes were the leading concern, cited by 65%, rising to 74% among Baby Boomers+ and falling to 50% among Gen Z. Generational differences are therefore visible in concern, with Baby Boomers+ reporting greater concern about misinformation and deepfakes than Gen Z. The wider climate is also more negative in Great Britain, where 40% expressed negative sentiment about AI’s impact on everyday life over the next decade, compared with 27% across all surveyed markets. These findings are survey responses collected during the stated fieldwork period, and cross-market comparisons use an unweighted average across 15 markets.

Decision brief

Great Britain’s generative-AI media landscape faces a trust and legitimacy gap, with concern and low news trust alongside strong demand for disclosure and stronger regulation.

  • Low trust creates a legitimacy barrier for AI-generated news in Great Britain, limiting the value of adoption unless credibility is protected.
  • Disclosure is a broad prerequisite for credible AI-mediated content, not a niche preference limited to particular markets or audiences.
  • Concern about misinformation is a central adoption risk, with older audiences likely to require stronger reassurance and safeguards than younger audiences.

05. Expert view

MarketBearing expert view.

Our team's perspective translates the report into practical implications for business, brand and digital leaders.

MB

MarketBearing analysis

Great Britain’s AI media challenge is a legitimacy gap, not simply a format issue. 70% say they trust AI-generated news less than human-created news, while discomfort remains at majority levels across social media posts, written articles, and video. This places the trust constraint across distribution formats rather than in one channel, alongside an 86% expectation that AI use should be disclosed. For decision-makers, transparency should therefore be treated as a baseline operating condition for AI-mediated content, not a niche feature. The requirement is also unevenly experienced: concern about misinformation and deepfakes is 65% nationally, compared with 50% among Gen Z and 74% among Baby Boomers+, while 70% say there are not enough regulations. That sits within a more cautious Great Britain market, where negative sentiment about AI’s role in everyday life is 40% versus 27% across all surveyed markets, and only 19% trust AI-generated news equally or more compared with 37% across all markets. Leaders should combine visible disclosure and attention to regulatory expectations with audience-specific approaches, rather than importing higher-acceptance assumptions or applying one national response.
in LinkedIn-ready post
Trust is becoming the defining constraint on AI-generated news in Great Britain. That is one representative angle from YouGov’s report, Trust or trepidation?: How Brits feel about generative AI in media. Seventy percent of people in Great Britain say they trust AI-generated news less than human-created news. Only 19% trust it equally or more, compared with 37% across all surveyed markets. The concern is broader than trust in a single article. YouGov identifies misinformation and deepfakes as the leading concern about generative AI, and the majority of Brits are uncomfortable with AI being used across every tested news format, including social posts, written articles, video, imagery, and audio. Acceptance cannot simply be assumed to transfer from one format or channel to another. Transparency is therefore a baseline expectation, not an optional reassurance. Eighty-six percent consider disclosure of generative AI use important, and support exceeds 75% in every surveyed market. The findings suggest that credible use depends on legitimacy being designed into the experience: clear disclosure, human oversight, provenance, and format-specific trust testing should sit alongside production-efficiency assessments. For media and digital operators, the implication is specific. Scaling AI-generated news without protecting credibility risks overlooking the audience condition that matters most: whether people are prepared to trust what they receive.
