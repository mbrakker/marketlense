TL;DR

Social video time continues to grow in the U.S., but growth is decelerating. YouTube maintains the broadest reach across age cohorts. TikTok and Instagram are projected to lead ad revenue growth among social video platforms. Per-user monetization is highest on Instagram and Facebook, with some platforms showing declining ARPU into 2026E.

5 Insights

  1. Average daily social video time per U.S. adult rose from 0:20 (2019) to 0:52 (2024E) and is forecast to reach 0:57 by 2028E; 2019–2024E CAGR = 21%, slowing to 2% for 2024E–2028E.
  2. YouTube has the broadest reach across age cohorts; the chart indicates ~211M U.S. monthly users in 2024 (inferred from totals; attribution and exact figure may reflect rounding—uncertainty acknowledged).
  3. TikTok and Instagram are forecast to be the fastest-growing social video ad businesses through 2026; the top 2024E–2026E CAGR shown is ~16% vs ~6–10% for others (exact platform-to-CAGR mapping is partially obscured—uncertainty).
  4. Instagram and Facebook lead in monthly ad revenue per user in 2024E at roughly ~$20 and ~$16, respectively, while mid-tier platforms are around ~$8–$10 and smaller ones near ~$2.25.
  5. Multiple platforms’ per-user ad revenue are projected to edge down by 2026E (e.g., ~$20.02→$16.74; ~$15.96→$14.15; ~$2.25→$1.99), signaling monetization pressure despite rising usage.

Commentary

For brands, the mix should tilt toward social video, but decelerating time growth and signs of ARPU compression require sharper creative, tighter audience targeting, and rigorous incrementality testing to protect ROAS. Prioritize TikTok and Instagram for growth, while leveraging YouTube’s broad reach for scaled outcomes. Expect CPM and competition to intensify; manage frequency caps, diversify placements, and harmonize MMM with geo experiments and attention metrics to verify lift. Creative iteration native to each platform and first‑party data activation will be essential to sustain efficiency as monetization dynamics shift.
